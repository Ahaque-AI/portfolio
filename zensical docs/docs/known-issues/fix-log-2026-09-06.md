# Smoother orbits and guided-flight scene foundation

## Symptom and cause

Orbit motion felt uneven. The introduction traveler used wall time, jumping
ahead after hidden/offscreen pauses. Map parallax used a fixed interpolation
per frame, changing response with refresh rate. Its signal wrapped from the
route end directly to the start. Introduction tilt affected only the SVG,
separating its annotation from the WebGL geometry.

## Resolution

Use a paused elapsed clock for the traveler, reset map timestamps on resume,
and use elapsed-time exponential damping for map parallax. The signal now
eases along the route and returns continuously. Apply introduction tilt to
the shared SVG/canvas wrapper, including the reduced-motion override.

Implemented [guided-flight Step 02](../plans/guided-flight-solar-system.md):
reusable solar-system and rocket geometry, three planet stops, deterministic
stars, sparse comet, mouse parallax, visibility/reduced-motion handling and
resource disposal. The Step 03 overlay is not part of this change.

## Guided experience and asteroid encounters

The guided flight felt like a conventional step dialog and the ship resembled a
pencil. The flight now uses asteroid encounters at every stop, a bounded data
stream, and a compact exploration craft with cockpit, wings and twin engines.
Each map visit spotlights the launch control until the visitor starts the
flight. It stores no information. Shared orbital
ring feedback now applies to controls and destinations, while every internal
route uses the WebGL orbit transition where motion is available.

Verification: scene tests assert asteroid fields, encounter motion, ship parts,

## Guided game controls and resume presentation

The flight still resembled a tutorial because its next, back and landing actions
were visible in the game. It now keeps Back to map plus two explicit sector
controls: Up advances one encounter and Down returns one. The shooter view
removes orbital route marks, puts full spherical
section planets at different lateral and depth positions. The swept-wing
four-engine fighter and its following camera travel between them, so asteroid
fields pass the player during each approach. Its pulsing engines and cyan bolt
stream aim at the active field, with a non-interactive targeting reticle over
the scene. Planet colors are red, teal, violet and blue. The encounter panel
alternates sides. The game dialog locks its
viewport so it cannot show a page scrollbar. The launch control now has dark text on a solid citron
background. Its focus veil was reduced to a low-opacity 1.8px blur and returns
on each unlaunched map visit so the route map stays visible while launch remains
the clear next action. The ship now uses broad wings, a dark cockpit and four
thrusters for a game craft silhouette.

The prior CV was a portfolio reading page with a rail and many links. The first
replacement then clipped its contents by scaling an A4 sheet into the viewport.
It now uses the owner-supplied PDF as its content and hierarchy reference while
keeping the portfolio's dark palette and local display and body fonts. The full
resume is readable through normal document scrolling with no site chrome or
resume navigation. The sheet was then tightened to a 980px maximum with smaller
type and spacing so the opening screen shows more resume information at once.

## Centered sector travel and control reliability

The sector buttons appeared over the scene but did not consistently receive
pointer input. They now sit above every scene layer with direct pointer and
touch behavior, plus keyboard activation. ArrowUp and ArrowDown also work while
focus remains on Back to map. A transition now hides non-selected
planets and continuously targets the selected planet with the camera so the
planet, ship and reticle return to the center together. The camera depth range
now includes sections three and four, travel duration scales with path length,
and the departing planet remains visible during the handoff. Camera and look-at
settling use damped interpolation to remove snapping between frames. The fighter
silhouette is larger with brighter exhausts and forward cannons.
